# Traditions — Outline

One section per tradition, in a fixed template:

- **Scope**: sub-traditions covered.
- **Core texts**: the texts to ingest.
- **Languages**: what the originals are written in.
- **Digital sources**: where the data comes from.
- **Motifs and themes**: what to tag.
- **Seed leads**: starting candidates, each with its relation type
  (inheritance / transmission / convergence / response). Leads marked
  *contested* must be surfaced with that label.
- **Caveats**.

Islam is the reference point for most seed leads, but the pipeline compares
every pair of traditions.

## WESTERN

### Ancient Near East

- **Scope:** Sumerian, Akkadian (Babylonian/Assyrian), Ugaritic/Canaanite,
  Egyptian, Hittite/Hurrian.
- **Core texts:** Eridu Genesis; Enki and Ninhursag; Atrahasis; Gilgamesh;
  Enuma Elish; Baal Cycle; Sargon Birth Legend; Instruction of Amenemope;
  Great Hymn to the Aten; Tale of Two Brothers; Kumarbi cycle; Illuyanka;
  Hittite treaties and plague prayers.
- **Languages:** Sumerian, Akkadian, Ugaritic, Egyptian, Hittite, Hurrian.
- **Digital sources:**
  - ETCSL, ePSD2, ORACC, eBL, CDLI (Sumerian and Akkadian)
  - TLA (Egyptian)
  - DULAT (Ugaritic lexicon)
  - TLHdig / Hethitologie-Portal Mainz, eDiAna (Hittite and Anatolian)
- **Motifs and themes:** flood hero; primordial waters and chaos combat; the
  storm god vs. the sea or a serpent; garden or paradise land; divine council;
  the infant set adrift; wisdom instruction; vassal treaty form.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Transmission:* Sargon Birth Legend ↔ Moses in the basket (Exodus 2;
    Quran 20:39, 28:7). This shares the *yamm* passages.
  - *Transmission:* Instruction of Amenemope ↔ Proverbs 22:17–24:22.
  - *Transmission:* Tablet XI of Gilgamesh and Atrahasis ↔ Genesis 6–9 ↔
    Quran's Noah.
  - *Inheritance/transmission:* Baal vs. Yam, and Lotan ↔ Leviathan.
  - *Inheritance/transmission:* Enuma Elish's Tiamat ↔ *tehom* ("the deep").
    The etymology is debated.
  - *Transmission:* Hittite vassal treaties ↔ Deuteronomy.
  - *Transmission:* Kumarbi ↔ Hesiod's *Theogony*.
  - *Convergence or transmission, debated:* Hymn to the Aten ↔ Psalm 104.
  - *Transmission, debated:* Tale of Two Brothers ↔ Joseph and Potiphar's
    wife (Quran 12).
  - *Lexical loan chains:* *guza* → *kussû* → *kursī*; *é-gal* → *ekallu* →
    *haykal*; *tinūru* → *tannūr*.
- **Caveats:**
  - Texts are fragmentary and composite, and many have several versions;
    dates are often uncertain.
  - Ugaritic texts are less openly available.

### Greco-Roman

- **Scope:** Archaic myth, classical philosophy, Hellenistic and late antique
  religion (Hermetism, mystery cults, Neoplatonism), Greek romance and
  hagiography.
- **Core texts:** Hesiod; Homer; Orphic fragments; Plato (*Timaeus*,
  *Republic*); Plotinus; Proclus; *Corpus Hermeticum*; Ovid's
  *Metamorphoses*; Alexander Romance; Seven Sleepers of Ephesus (Greek and
  Syriac).
- **Languages:** Greek and Latin, plus their Syriac and Arabic translations.
- **Digital sources:**
  - Perseus / Scaife, First1KGreek, The Latin Library
  - Glossarium Graeco-Arabicum (Bochum), a word-by-word record of the
    Greek → Arabic translation movement
- **Motifs and themes:** flood (Deucalion); the first woman and the origin of
  evil (Pandora); hospitality to disguised gods (Baucis and Philemon);
  descent to the underworld; emanation, the One, and the intellect; the
  world soul; sleepers who wake centuries later; the world-conqueror king.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Transmission, contested in direction and dating:* Alexander Romance and
    the Syriac Alexander Legend ↔ Dhul-Qarnayn (Quran 18:83–98).
  - *Transmission:* Seven Sleepers ↔ the People of the Cave (Quran 18:9–26).
  - *Transmission:* Plotinus' *Enneads* IV–VI → the Arabic *Theology of
    Aristotle* → Islamic philosophy and Sufism.
  - *Response:* Hermes Trismegistus identified with Idris in Islamic
    tradition.
  - *Convergence or transmission:* Baucis and Philemon ↔ Abraham's guests,
    and the destruction of Lot's city (Genesis 18–19; Quran 11, 51).
  - *Convergence:* Deucalion ↔ the Near Eastern floods.
  - *Convergence:* Pandora ↔ Eve.
- **Caveats:**
  - The TLG is paywalled.
  - Late antique Syriac intermediaries matter more than the Greek originals
    for the Quran-facing leads.

### Dualist

#### Zoroastrianism

- **Scope:** Old and Young Avestan scripture; Middle Persian (Pahlavi)
  literature; modern Parsi and Irani communities.
- **Core texts:** Gathas; Yasna; Yashts; Vendidad; Bundahishn; Denkard;
  *Arda Viraf Namag*; *Zand-i Wahman Yasn*.
- **Languages:** Avestan, Middle Persian, Pazand.
- **Digital sources:**
  - TITUS, Avestan Digital Archive
  - avesta.org (largely public-domain translations from *Sacred Books of the
    East*)
- **Motifs and themes:** cosmic dualism (Ahura Mazda vs. Angra Mainyu); the
  bridge of judgment (Chinvat); a tour of heaven and hell; the final savior
  (Saoshyant); bodily resurrection and the renovation of the world
  (frashokereti); the Amesha Spentas; ritual purity; fire.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Inheritance:* Indo-Iranian vocabulary shared with the Vedas: the roles of
    *deva*/*daēva* and *asura*/*ahura* are inverted; *Mitra*/*Mithra*;
    *soma*/*haoma*; *ṛta*/*aša*.
  - *Transmission or convergence, contested:* Chinvat ↔ the *ṣirāṭ*.
  - *Transmission or convergence, contested:* *Arda Viraf* ↔ the *Miʿrāj*.
  - *Transmission or convergence:* Saoshyant ↔ the messianic figure ↔ the
    Mahdi.
  - *Lexical:* the *Majūs* (Quran 22:17).
- **Caveats:**
  - Most Pahlavi texts were written down after the Islamic conquest, so the
    direction of influence is often unclear.
  - This is a living community.

#### Manichaeism

- **Scope:** the Roman West (Egypt, North Africa) and the East (Iran, Central
  Asia, China).
- **Core texts:** *Kephalaia*; the Psalm-Book; the Cologne Mani Codex; the
  Turfan fragments; the Chinese Hymnscroll and Compendium; the *Shābuhragān*;
  hostile sources (Augustine; Ibn al-Nadim's *Fihrist*).
- **Languages:** Coptic, Greek, Syriac, Middle Persian, Parthian, Sogdian,
  Uyghur, Chinese, Latin, Arabic.
- **Digital sources:**
  - Digitales Turfan-Archiv
  - Gardner & Lieu, Klimkeit (translations under copyright; personal use only)
- **Motifs and themes:** Light vs. Darkness; particles of light trapped in
  matter; the Jesus of splendour; the Living Soul; the Five Elements; the seal
  of the prophets; the elect and the hearers.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Response:* Mani presented as the "Buddha of Light" in Chinese and Uyghur
    texts, and as the Apostle of Jesus Christ in the West.
  - *Response:* the Fihrist as an Islamic window onto Manichaeism; the
    persecution of *zindīqs*.
  - *Transmission or response, contested:* Mani as "seal of the prophets" ↔
    Quran 33:40.
  - *Transmission:* a Manichaean intermediary for the Barlaam and Josaphat
    story.
- **Caveats:**
  - The texts are fragmentary and scattered across many scripts.
  - Many key translations are copyrighted.

#### Mandaeism

- **Scope:** the classical texts and the living Mandaean community (Iraq,
  Iran, diaspora).
- **Core texts:** Ginza Rba (Right and Left); the Book of John (*Drashe
  d-Yahya*); the Qolasta / Canonical Prayerbook; *Haran Gawaita*; *Diwan
  Abatur*.
- **Languages:** Classical Mandaic (Eastern Aramaic).
- **Digital sources:**
  - Lidzbarski's German translations (public domain)
  - The Drower–Macuch dictionary
  - Gelbert's English translations (copyright)
- **Motifs and themes:** the World of Light vs. the Darkness; the uthras;
  Ruha; baptism in living water (*maṣbuta*); John the Baptist; the ascent of
  the soul through watch-houses.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Inheritance:* the Aramaic lexical track, e.g. Mandaic *yama* ↔ *yamm*.
  - *Response or convergence:* Yahya (John the Baptist; Quran 19) as a central
    figure.
  - *Contested identification:* the Sabians (Quran 2:62, 5:69, 22:17) ↔ the
    Mandaeans.
  - *Convergence or transmission:* the ascent of the soul ↔ the Manichaean
    and Gnostic ascents.
- **Caveats:**
  - This is a living, persecuted community, and some material is esoteric.
    Use only published texts.

### Covenantal

#### Judaism

- **Scope:** the Hebrew Bible; Second Temple literature; rabbinic literature;
  Judeo-Arabic; medieval mysticism.
- **Core texts:** Tanakh; Targums; Mishnah; the two Talmuds; midrash (Genesis
  Rabbah, *Pirqei de-Rabbi Eliezer*, Tanhuma); Hekhalot; the Zohar; Saadia
  Gaon's Arabic *Tafsīr*; the Cairo Genizah.
- **Languages:** Hebrew, Aramaic, Judeo-Arabic.
- **Digital sources:** ETCBC/BHSA (via Text-Fabric), OSHB, Sefaria (API),
  Dicta, Friedberg Genizah Project, CAL.
- **Motifs and themes:** covenant; exodus; prophets; the golden calf; Cain and
  Abel; Abraham against idolatry; Joseph; Solomon and the jinn or demons;
  the heavenly ascent.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Transmission:* Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5, "whoever saves one life…", ↔ Quran
    5:32. The Quran frames it as decreed for the Children of Israel.
  - *Transmission:* Cain and the raven (Quran 5:31) ↔ *Pirqei de-Rabbi
    Eliezer* 21. In the Jewish version, Adam learns to bury from the raven.
  - *Transmission:* Abraham smashes the idols (Quran 21:51–70) ↔ Genesis
    Rabbah 38:13.
  - *Response:* Saadia's Judeo-Arabic translation uses Islamic theological
    vocabulary.
  - *Inheritance:* all Hebrew–Arabic cognates (*yam*/*yamm*…).
- **Caveats:**
  - Rabbinic dating is often later than the traditions it records, so the
    direction of influence must be argued, not assumed.

#### Christianity

Christianity is covered from its origins to the present. Its branches and
eras are treated as five sub-traditions. Material from after Islam's rise
(roughly the 7th century) is compared mainly at the level of philosophy and
metaphysics: concepts rather than words or stories. For those periods, the
expected relation types are **response** and **convergence**, plus
**reverse transmission** from the Islamic world into Christian thought.

**Shared across all periods**
- **Languages:** Greek, Syriac, Latin, Coptic, Ge'ez, Armenian, Arabic, then
  the European vernaculars.
- **Motifs and concepts:** the virgin birth; Mary; miracles; the disciples;
  the crucifixion and docetism; the Paraclete; Satan's refusal to bow to
  Adam; the Trinity and polemics against it; the Logos; divine attributes and
  divine simplicity; mystical union; prophecy and revelation; grace and free
  will.

##### Early Christianity (to about the 5th century), including the Gnostics

- **Scope:** the New Testament and early apocrypha; Jewish-Christian groups
  (Ebionites, Elchasaites); the Gnostics (Sethians, Valentinians, Basilides);
  Marcion; the early Fathers.
- **Core texts:**
  - Scripture and early writings: the New Testament; the Septuagint; the
    Diatessaron.
  - Infancy narratives: the Protevangelium of James; the Infancy Gospel of
    Thomas; Pseudo-Matthew; the Arabic/Syriac Infancy Gospel.
  - Adam literature: the *Life of Adam and Eve*.
  - The Nag Hammadi library: the Gospel of Thomas; the Apocryphon of John;
    the Gospel of Truth; the Second Treatise of the Great Seth.
  - Jewish-Christian literature: the Pseudo-Clementines.
  - Heresiologists: Irenaeus, Hippolytus, Epiphanius.
- **Digital sources:**
  - New Testament and Septuagint: MorphGNT/SBLGNT, CATSS.
  - Apocrypha: e-Clavis (NASSCAL).
  - Coptic: Coptic Scriptorium (includes some Nag Hammadi material).
  - Translations: CCEL / New Advent (*Ante-Nicene Fathers*, public domain).
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Transmission:* the Infancy Gospel of Thomas (Jesus brings clay birds to
    life) ↔ Quran 3:49 and 5:110.
  - *Transmission:* the Protevangelium of James (Mary raised in the Temple;
    Zechariah; casting lots) ↔ Quran 3:35–44.
  - *Transmission:* Pseudo-Matthew (the palm tree on the flight to Egypt) ↔
    Quran 19:23–26.
  - *Transmission:* the Arabic/Syriac Infancy Gospel (Jesus speaks in the
    cradle) ↔ Quran 19:29–33 and 3:46.
  - *Transmission:* the *Life of Adam and Eve* (Satan refuses to worship
    Adam) ↔ Quran 2:34, 7:11–12, and 38:71–76.
  - *Contested:* the crucifixion denial (Quran 4:157) ↔ the Second Treatise
    of the Great Seth, and the substitution of Simon of Cyrene reported of
    Basilides.
  - *Contested:* the Pseudo-Clementine "True Prophet" doctrine and the
    Jewish-Christian hypothesis ↔ the Quran's view of prophets.
  - *Transmission:* the Elchasaites ↔ Mani's upbringing (Cologne Mani Codex)
    → Manichaeism.
  - *Convergence or transmission:* the cosmology of the Apocryphon of John ↔
    the Mandaean and Manichaean cosmologies.
  - *Contested:* Aḥmad (Quran 61:6) ↔ the Paraclete (*paraklētos* vs.
    *periklytos*).

##### Eastern Christianity

- **Scope:**
  - Syriac Christianity (the West Syriac church and the Church of the East);
    the highest priority for Quran-facing leads.
  - The Byzantine/Orthodox tradition.
  - The Coptic, Ethiopian, and Armenian churches.
  - Arabic Christianity.
- **Core texts:**
  - Syriac: the Peshitta; the hymns of Ephrem; the homilies of Jacob of
    Serugh; the *Cave of Treasures*; the Acts of Thomas (Hymn of the Pearl);
    the Syriac Alexander Legend.
  - Christian–Muslim encounter: John of Damascus; Timothy I's dialogue with
    the caliph al-Mahdi; Theodore Abu Qurrah.
  - Byzantine mysticism: the *Philokalia*; Gregory Palamas.
  - Ethiopian: the *Kebra Nagast*.
- **Digital sources:** the ETCBC Peshitta (via Text-Fabric); the Digital
  Syriac Corpus (Beth Mardutho); Syriaca.org; Coptic Scriptorium; CCEL
  (*Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*).
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Transmission:* the *Cave of Treasures* (Satan refuses to bow to Adam) ↔
    Quran 2:34 and its parallels.
  - *Transmission:* Jacob of Serugh's homily on the Sleepers ↔ Quran 18.
  - *Transmission, contested in direction:* the Syriac Alexander Legend ↔
    Dhul-Qarnayn.
  - *Lexical:*
    - Syriac *ṣlōtā* ↔ *ṣalāt*.
    - Ge'ez *ḥawārya* ↔ *ḥawāriyyūn* ("disciples").
    - Syriac *qeryānā* ↔ *qurʾān* (proposed).
  - *Proposed:* the table from heaven (Quran 5:112–115) ↔ the Eucharist and
    the feeding miracles.
  - *Response:* John of Damascus on the "heresy of the Ishmaelites"; Timothy
    I and al-Mahdi; Abu Qurrah's Arabic theology.
  - *Convergence, or transmission (contested):* the hesychast Jesus Prayer ↔
    Sufi *dhikr*.
  - *Convergence or response:* Palamas' distinction between God's essence
    and energies ↔ the *kalām* debate over divine attributes.
  - *Response or contact:* Ethiopia and the first hijra to Abyssinia; the
    *Kebra Nagast*'s Solomon and Sheba ↔ Quran 27.
- **Caveats:**
  - Claims that the Quran is a Syriac text underneath, in the style of
    Luxenberg, are fringe and must be labeled as such.

##### Western Christianity (Latin, medieval to early modern)

- **Scope:** Latin patristics; scholasticism; medieval mysticism; the
  Reformation and the radical Reformation; Christian esotericism.
- **Core texts:**
  - Patristic and scholastic: Augustine; Aquinas; Meister Eckhart; Nicholas
    of Cusa (*De pace fidei*, *Cribratio Alkorani*).
  - Encounter with Islam: Ramon Llull; the *Liber Scalae Machometi* (Book of
    the Ladder); Robert of Ketton's Latin Quran (1143); Dante.
  - Reformation era: Luther on the Turks; Servetus; the Socinians.
  - Esoteric: the Rosicrucian manifestos.
- **Digital sources:** Corpus Thomisticum (Aquinas, lemmatized), Llull DB,
  The Latin Library, CCEL, Project Gutenberg.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Reverse transmission:* Avicenna, Averroes, and al-Ghazali in Aquinas and
    the scholastics.
  - *Response:* Llull's *Book of the Lover and the Beloved*. Llull himself
    names Sufi models for it.
  - *Transmission, contested:* the *Liber Scalae* (the Miʿrāj) → Dante's
    *Commedia*.
  - *Convergence:* Meister Eckhart ↔ Ibn ʿArabi.
  - *Response:* Cusanus' *Cribratio Alkorani* and *De pace fidei*.
  - *Convergence or response:* anti-Trinitarians (Servetus, the Socinians) ↔
    *tawḥīd*.
  - *Response or legend:* the Rosicrucian founder said to have studied in
    Damascus and Fez (*Fama Fraternitatis*).

##### Newer movements (18th century onward)

- **Scope:** Swedenborgianism, Latter-day Saints (Mormons), Jehovah's
  Witnesses, Christadelphians, Christian Science, Unitarian Universalism,
  Quakers, Pentecostalism.
- **Core texts:** Swedenborg's works; the Book of Mormon and the Doctrine and
  Covenants; the founding writings of each movement.
- **Digital sources:** the Joseph Smith Papers (open); the Swedenborg
  Foundation texts; Project Gutenberg.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Convergence:* new scripture brought by a final or restoring prophet (LDS)
    ↔ Islamic prophetology.
  - *Convergence:* non-Trinitarian monotheism (Jehovah's Witnesses,
    Christadelphians, Unitarians) ↔ *tawḥīd*.
  - *Convergence:* the Quaker "Inner Light" ↔ the Sufi *nūr*.
  - *Convergence:* Swedenborg's spirit-world visions ↔ the Miʿrāj and Islamic
    eschatology.

##### Modern philosophy and theology

- **Scope:** Christian-rooted philosophy and theology from the 18th century
  on: Hegel, Kierkegaard, Tillich, Teilhard de Chardin, Whitehead and process
  theology, and Christian existentialism.
- **Digital sources:** SKS (Kierkegaard, open), Project Gutenberg; many 20th
  century works are under copyright.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Response:* Muhammad Iqbal's *Reconstruction of Religious Thought in
    Islam* engaging Bergson, Nietzsche, and contemporary Western philosophy.
  - *Convergence:* Teilhard's Omega Point ↔ Islamic perfected-human
    (*al-insān al-kāmil*) cosmology.
  - *Convergence:* Tillich's "ground of being" ↔ Ibn ʿArabi's oneness of
    being (*waḥdat al-wujūd*).
  - *Convergence:* Kierkegaard's leap of faith ↔ al-Ghazali's *Deliverance
    from Error*.
- **Caveats:**
  - These comparisons are conceptual, so specificity scoring matters even
    more here. Generic resemblances ("both value faith") must be filtered out.

#### Samaritanism

- **Scope:** the Samaritan Pentateuch tradition and later Samaritan
  literature; a living community at Mount Gerizim/Nablus and Holon.
- **Core texts:** the Samaritan Pentateuch; the Samaritan Targum; *Tibat
  Marqe* (*Memar Marqe*); the Samaritan Arabic Pentateuch (Abu Saʿid); the
  Samaritan chronicles.
- **Languages:** Samaritan Hebrew, Samaritan Aramaic, Arabic.
- **Digital sources:** critical editions (Tal & Florentin; Schorch's
  edition project). Open data is limited.
- **Motifs and themes:** Mount Gerizim as the chosen place; Moses as the sole
  prophet; the Taheb (the "returning one"); purity.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Lexical and response, contested:* *al-Sāmirī* and the golden calf (Quran
    20:85–97).
  - *Convergence or transmission:* the Taheb ↔ the messianic figure / the
    Mahdi.
  - *Response:* the Samaritan Pentateuch's variants (Gerizim in the
    Decalogue) as a polemic against Jerusalem.
- **Caveats:**
  - The corpus is small and less digitized. The community is small and
    living.

## CENTRAL

### Sikhism

- **Scope:** Guru Granth Sahib, with an emphasis on its *bhagat bani*
  (compositions by non-Sikh saints, including Sufis); the Dasam Granth; the
  janamsakhis.
- **Core texts:** Guru Granth Sahib (the compositions of Sheikh Farid, Kabir,
  Namdev, Ravidas, and others); the Dasam Granth (including the *Zafarnama*);
  the janamsakhis of Guru Nanak; the *vārs* of Bhai Gurdas.
- **Languages:** Sant Bhasha, Old Punjabi, Braj, and Persian-influenced and
  Sanskrit-influenced registers, written in Gurmukhi; the *Zafarnama* is in
  Persian.
- **Digital sources:** BaniDB / SikhiToTheMax (open API); srigranth.org
  (with English translations).
- **Motifs and themes:** the oneness of God (*Ik Onkar*); the divine Name
  (*Nām*); the divine order (*hukam*, from Arabic *ḥukm*); the lover and the
  Beloved; death and the transience of life; criticism of external ritual.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Response:* Sheikh Farid's *shabads* and *saloks* in the Guru Granth
    Sahib (Chishti Sufism). Which Farid wrote them is debated, and the
    Gurus' responses to his saloks sit beside them.
  - *Lexical:* Arabic and Persian vocabulary in the Guru Granth Sahib
    (*Allāh*, *Raḥīm*, *Karīm*, *khalq*, *hukam*).
  - *Response:* the *Zafarnama*, Guru Gobind Singh's Persian letter to
    Aurangzeb.
  - *Response:* the janamsakhi story of Guru Nanak at Mecca.
  - *Convergence or transmission:* Kabir between Sufism and Bhakti.
- **Caveats:**
  - Attributions within the bhagat bani are debated.
  - Translations differ significantly on theological terms.

### Jainism

- **Scope:** the Śvetāmbara and Digambara canons and the shared philosophical
  literature.
- **Core texts:** the Śvetāmbara Āgamas (e.g., *Ācārāṅga*, *Sūtrakṛtāṅga*,
  *Kalpa Sūtra*); for the Digambaras, the *Ṣaṭkhaṇḍāgama* and Kundakunda;
  Umāsvāti's *Tattvārtha Sūtra*, which both accept; the Jain Rāmāyaṇa and
  Mahābhārata versions.
- **Languages:** Ardhamāgadhī and other Prakrits, Sanskrit.
- **Digital sources:** jainelibrary.org; GRETIL (partial); *Sacred Books of
  the East* vols. 22 and 45 (Jacobi, public domain).
- **Motifs and themes:** nonviolence (*ahiṃsā*); the many-sidedness of truth
  (*anekāntavāda*); karma as a subtle substance; the cosmos shaped as a cosmic
  person; the Tīrthaṅkaras; renunciation and fasting to death (*sallekhanā*);
  cycles of cosmic time.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Response:* the Jain retellings of the Hindu epics.
  - *Response:* Hiravijaya Suri at Akbar's court and the Jain–Mughal
    exchange.
  - *Convergence:* ascetic and animal-ethics themes ↔ Manichaean electi
    rules and ↔ the Sufi treatment of the lower self.
- **Caveats:**
  - The two sects disagree on which canon is authoritative.
  - Machine-readable Prakrit texts are scarce.

### Hinduism

- **Scope:** Vedic, Upanishadic, epic, Purāṇic, and Bhakti traditions, and
  Indo-Persian engagement with them.
- **Core texts:** Rigveda (including the *Nāsadīya* and *Puruṣa Sūkta*);
  *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa*; the Upaniṣads; the Mahābhārata (with the *Bhagavad
  Gītā*); the Rāmāyaṇa; the Purāṇas; the *Yoga Sūtras*; Bhakti poetry.
- **Languages:** Vedic and Classical Sanskrit, regional vernaculars, and
  Persian and Arabic translations.
- **Digital sources:** Digital Corpus of Sanskrit (lemmatized), GRETIL,
  SARIT, *Sacred Books of the East*.
- **Motifs and themes:** the flood (Manu and the fish / Matsya); cosmogony
  (the cosmic egg, the sacrifice of the primordial person, the creation
  hymn); cosmic order (*ṛta*/*dharma*); avatars; the unity of self and
  ultimate reality (*ātman*–*brahman*); devotion; cosmic time (*yugas*).
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Inheritance:* Indo-Iranian terms shared with the Avesta (see
    Zoroastrianism).
  - *Convergence:* Manu and the fish (*Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* 1.8.1) ↔ the Near
    Eastern floods.
  - *Response:* Dara Shikoh's *Sirr-i Akbar* (the Upaniṣads in Persian) and
    *Majmaʿ al-Baḥrayn*.
  - *Response:* al-Biruni's *Kitāb Pātanjal* (the *Yoga Sūtras* in Arabic)
    and his *Taḥqīq mā li-l-Hind*.
  - *Transmission:* the *Amṛtakuṇḍa* → the Arabic/Persian *Ḥawḍ al-ḥayāt*.
  - *Transmission chain:* the *Sirr-i Akbar* → Anquetil-Duperron's Latin
    *Oupnek'hat* → European thought.
- **Caveats:**
  - Vedic dating is uncertain.
  - The corpus is enormous, so start with the Vedas, the Brāhmaṇa flood
    story, the Upaniṣads, the Gītā, and the Indo-Persian translations.

## EASTERN

### Buddhism

- **Scope:** Theravāda (Pali), the Chinese canon, the Tibetan canon, and the
  Gandhāran and Central Asian transmission.
- **Core texts:** the Pali Tipiṭaka; the Chinese Āgamas; Mahāyāna sūtras
  (Lotus, Heart, Pure Land); the Jātakas; the *Milindapañha*; the Tibetan
  Kangyur and Tengyur.
- **Languages:** Pali, Sanskrit, Gāndhārī, Chinese, Tibetan, Sogdian, Uyghur.
- **Digital sources:**
  - SuttaCentral (already maps parallels across Pali, Chinese, Sanskrit, and
    Tibetan)
  - CBETA (Chinese)
  - 84000 and BDRC (Tibetan)
- **Motifs and themes:** the renouncing prince; the four sights; the future
  Buddha (Maitreya); rebirth and karma; the bodhisattva; the Pure Land
  paradise; past-life tales.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Transmission:* the Buddha's life → the Arabic *Bilawhar wa-Būdhāsf* →
    the Georgian *Balavariani* → the Greek *Barlaam and Josaphat*.
  - *Response:* Rashid al-Din's *Life of the Buddha* in the *Jāmiʿ
    al-tawārīkh*.
  - *Response:* the *Milindapañha*, a Greek king questioning a Buddhist monk.
  - *Response:* Buddhist–Manichaean exchange at Turfan.
  - *Convergence or transmission:* Maitreya ↔ messianic figures.
  - *Contested, fringe:* Dhul-Kifl ↔ the Buddha (*Kifl* read as Kapilavastu).
- **Caveats:**
  - Sectarian canons differ.
  - SuttaCentral's parallel mappings are a ready-made benchmark for the motif
    track.

### Shinto

- **Scope:** the classical myth corpus, ritual texts, and Shinto–Buddhist
  combination.
- **Core texts:** *Kojiki* (712); *Nihon Shoki* (720); the norito prayers in
  the *Engishiki*; the *Fudoki*; the *Man'yōshū*.
- **Languages:** Old Japanese and Classical Chinese (kanbun).
- **Digital sources:** open editions of the originals (e.g., Wikisource);
  Chamberlain's *Kojiki* (1882) and Aston's *Nihongi* (1896) translations
  (public domain).
- **Motifs and themes:** the creating pair (Izanagi and Izanami); descent to
  the underworld (Yomi); the sun goddess hiding in a cave (Amaterasu); the
  storm god slaying a serpent (Susanoo and Yamata no Orochi); purification
  (*harae*, *misogi*); kami; sacred kingship.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Convergence:* Izanagi in Yomi ↔ Orpheus and Eurydice.
  - *Convergence:* Susanoo vs. Orochi ↔ Illuyanka, Baal vs. Yam/Lotan, and
    Leviathan.
  - *Convergence:* purification by water ↔ Islamic ablution (*wuḍūʾ*) and
    Mandaean baptism.
  - *Response:* Shinto–Buddhist identification of kami with buddhas
    (*honji suijaku*).
- **Caveats:**
  - There was essentially no premodern contact with the Abrahamic religions,
    so leads are almost all convergence or East Asian response.

### Chinese religion (Confucianism, Daoism)

- **Scope:** the Confucian classics and Neo-Confucianism; the Daoist
  philosophical texts and the Daoist canon; popular religion; Abrahamic texts
  written in Chinese.
- **Core texts:**
  - The Five Classics and the Four Books; Zhu Xi's commentaries.
  - The *Daodejing*; the *Zhuangzi*; the *Daozang* (Daoist canon).
  - Han Kitab: Wang Daiyu's *Zhengjiao zhenquan*; Liu Zhi's *Tianfang
    xingli* and *Tianfang dianli*; Ma Zhu's *Qingzhen zhinan*.
  - Persian Sufi texts in Chinese: Liu Zhi's *Zhenjing zhaowei* (Jāmī's
    *Lawāʾiḥ*).
  - The Nestorian Stele (781); the Kaifeng Jewish steles (1489, 1512, 1663).
- **Languages:** Classical Chinese.
- **Digital sources:**
  - ctext.org (API), Kanripo (includes the full Daozang), CBETA
  - Han Kitab digitization is patchy. Murata, Chittick and Tu's translations
    are under copyright.
- **Motifs and themes:** the principle and vital energy of Neo-Confucianism
  (*li*, *qi*); the Supreme Ultimate and the Ultimateless (*taiji*,
  *wuji*); the Way (*Dao*); Heaven's mandate (*tianming*); the sage; the
  flood-taming hero (Yu the Great); filial piety; inner alchemy.
- **Seed leads:**
  - *Response:* the Han Kitab use 真一 "the Real One" and Neo-Confucian
    metaphysics to express *tawḥīd* (↔ Zhu Xi).
  - *Response:* the Nestorian Stele presents the Church of the East in Daoist
    and Buddhist idiom.
  - *Response:* the Kaifeng steles present Judaism in Confucian idiom.
  - *Response:* Chinese Manichaean texts written in Buddhist and Daoist
    idiom.
  - *Convergence:* Gun and Yu taming the flood ↔ flood narratives elsewhere.
  - *Convergence:* the Daoist "One" ↔ the Neoplatonic One.
- **Caveats:**
  - "Chinese religion" was syncretic in practice (the "Three Teachings").
  - Classical Chinese segmentation needs care.
