    the patients'' garment: may it release them to wellbeing and life and hide the evil away in an evil
    thing (weight 7.3); Seven copper (or bronze) nails are driven into the ground: may the evil be nailed
    down with its mouth and tongue, with the Sun''s help (weight 7.3); A sheep is declared the patients''
    nakkušši (carrier of evil) with mouth and tongue, presented to the Sun, but not killed: the Old Woman
    takes it for herself (weight 7.3); A salamander wrapped in blue and red wool is waved over the patients:
    may its swift foot carry off the evil tongue on its back and with its teeth (weight 7.3); Seven vessels
    filled with wine, oil, honey, fruit, sinew, salt and fat are emptied one by one into the hearth and
    broken: may it break with mouth and tongue (weight 7.3); The exalted Sun god is declared to stand
    behind (guarantee the treatment of) mouth and tongue (weight 7.3); The substitute animal is slaughtered
    down into a dug pit, laid in it with sweet bread and wine, and covered with earth (weight 7.1); An
    animal (sheep, black sheep, puppy) is held over the patients and declared their substitute (tarpalli-)
    for their heads and whole persons (weight 6.6). Pair score 57.7.'
  basis: reading
- text: 'Only in Maštigga ritual, clay and cumin, the salamander and puppy, Šaušga''s vessel, the carrier
    sheep and the seven nails (CTH 404.1.I §26-38): The patients place their hand on the bread, cheese
    and wine before the Old Woman breaks and offers it; Analogic spell: as this clay will not return to
    the riverbank, black cumin will not turn white nor become another seed, and this dough will not go
    into the gods'' bread, so may the evil tongue not go into the patients'' bodies; The patients spit
    into the mouth of the substitute animal: ''you have spat out the evil curses''; The Old Woman sprinkles
    the patients with water and waves dough over them: ''be pure again of mouth and tongue''; Living goat
    bears the people''s sins away into the wilderness.'
  basis: reading

    of Ḫanikkuili (CTH 404.1.II §20-44): A pot waved over the patients is called the substitute for their
    heads and broken; they smash another vessel with their feet, breaking all the evil words and curses;
